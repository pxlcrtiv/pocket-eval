# DeFi 101

Decentralized finance, or DeFi, replaces traditional intermediaries with
smart contracts on public blockchains. Instead of a bank matching lenders and
borrowers, an automated market maker pools assets and prices them with a
formula. Instead of a centralized exchange order book, a decentralized
exchange lets users swap tokens directly through a smart contract.

The core building blocks are tokens, liquidity pools, and smart contracts.
Tokens represent value and follow standards such as ERC-20 for fungible
assets. A liquidity pool holds reserves of two or more tokens and enables
trading without a counterparty. Anyone can provide liquidity and earn fees
from trades. Automated market makers use the constant product formula to
price assets. When the ratio of reserves changes, the price changes too.
Large trades move the price more than small trades. Slippage is the
difference between the expected price and the price actually received.

DeFi applications compose like Lego bricks. A user can deposit collateral,
borrow a stablecoin, swap it on a decentralized exchange, and provide
liquidity to a pool. Composability is powerful but also creates risk,
because one vulnerable contract can drain funds across many protocols.

Governance tokens give holders a vote in protocol decisions. Proposals can
change fees, upgrade contracts, or move treasury funds. Decentralized
autonomous organizations, or DAOs, coordinate this process on-chain with
voting and timelocks. The promise of DeFi is open access: anyone with an
internet connection and a wallet can participate without permission.
